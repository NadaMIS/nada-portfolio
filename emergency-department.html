Case study /01 Healthcare operations Team of 4 · my part: severity classification Python · SARIMA · Prophet · scikit-learn

When does the ER feel the pressure?

A year of emergency visits at the Royal Commission Hospital in Jubail (2022), used to forecast demand, predict how severe each case is, and group visits by how they move through the department.

90,036complete visits after cleaning (from 96,537)
21.3%daily forecast error with SARIMA (Prophet: 26.6%)
0.48macro F1 for severity, up from 0.32
3patient-flow groups with different capacity needs
Problem

Patient volume swings from day to day, waits average 33 minutes, and triage has to judge severity fast. The hospital needed to know when demand peaks, which patients are critical, and where capacity runs short.

Findings
My part

The more accurate model missed every critical patient.

The Decision Tree scored 75% accuracy by predicting the common levels. I balanced the training data with SMOTE and class weights; the Random Forest gave up 3 points of accuracy but caught 2 of the 5 Severity 1 cases and raised Severity 5 recall from 0.07 to 0.26.

Recall by severity level (test set)Decision TreeRandom Forest + SMOTE

SARIMA forecast daily arrivals better than Prophet.

Validated on the last 30 days. Prophet caught the weekly pattern but forecast too high every day. About 21% error is good enough for weekly rosters, not for day-level decisions.

Forecast error on the last 30 days · lower is better

Three groups, three different bottlenecks.

Clustering on wait time, length of stay and severity separated quick low-acuity visits from long high-acuity stays. The sicker the group, the shorter the wait and the longer the stay.

Average wait vs average stay by cluster (minutes)
Decision
  1. Staff to the curveShift coverage toward 10 AM – 4 PM and the busier weekdays.
  2. Judge triage models on recallMissing a critical case costs more than mislabelling a routine one.
  3. Plan capacity by groupTriage throughput, observation space and beds are three separate problems.
My role

I built the severity classification: class balancing, model comparison, and choosing the model on macro F1 and critical-case recall. Team: Nusaybah Aljaafari, Malak BaFadal, Aysha Saquib.

Limits

One hospital, one year. Severity 1 and 2 had only 20 and 320 training cases, so those predictions stay uncertain.
