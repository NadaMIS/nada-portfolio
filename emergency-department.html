Emergency Department Analytics

Forecasting daily demand, predicting how severe each patient is, and grouping visits by how they move through a hospital emergency department.

  • Forecasting
  • Machine learning
  • Healthcare
90,036ER visits analysed
21.3%daily forecast error with SARIMA
0.48macro F1 for severity, up from 0.32
3patient groups with different needs

The problem

Patient volume swings from day to day, waits average 33 minutes, and triage has to judge severity fast. The hospital needed to know when demand peaks, which patients are critical, and where capacity runs short.

My role

Team project with Nusaybah Aljaafari, Malak BaFadal and Aysha Saquib. I built the severity classification.

  • Python
  • pandas
  • scikit-learn
  • imbalanced-learn
  • statsmodels
  • Prophet

How I approached it

  1. Clean96,537 visits → 90,036 complete records
  2. ForecastSARIMA vs Prophet on the last 30 days
  3. ClassifySMOTE + class weights, two models compared
  4. SegmentGaussian Mixture clusters of patient flow

What I found

My part

The more accurate model missed every critical patient.

The Decision Tree scored 75% accuracy by predicting the common levels and caught no Severity 1 case. The Random Forest I balanced with SMOTE gave up 3 points of accuracy, caught 2 of 5 critical cases and raised Severity 5 recall from 0.07 to 0.26.

SARIMA forecast daily arrivals better than Prophet.

Validated on the last 30 days. About 21% error is good enough for weekly rosters, not for day-level decisions. Arrivals peak between 10 AM and 4 PM.

Three groups, three different bottlenecks.

The sicker the group, the shorter the wait and the longer the stay: front-desk queues, observation space and beds are separate capacity problems.

Recommendation

  1. Staff to the curveShift coverage toward 10 AM – 4 PM and the busier weekdays.
  2. Judge triage models on recallMissing a critical case costs more than mislabelling a routine one.
  3. Plan capacity by groupTriage, observation and beds need separate plans.

Limits

Limits: One hospital and one year of data. Severity 1 and 2 had only 20 and 320 training cases, so those predictions stay uncertain.
