Mostakan Tea Room

A tea room in Al-Hofuf was losing customers. We turned its SAR 5,000 monthly marketing budget into an optimization problem across five social platforms.

  • Optimization
  • Business analysis
  • Client work

The problem

Most of the café's 20 products lost profit year over year while operating costs stayed flat, so the problem was fewer customers, not higher costs. The owner's plan to bake in-house would help margins but not bring people in.

My role

Joint team project with Nusaybah Aljaafari, Saud Alshammari and Taghreed Al Qahtani. All four of us worked on every stage.

  • Excel Solver
  • Linear programming
  • Sensitivity analysis

How I approached it

  1. Diagnosesales and orders, 2023–2025
  2. Size the marketlocal users per platform
  3. Modelmaximize reach under budget limits
  4. Stress-testshadow prices and budget scenarios

What I found

Two platforms take 82% of the budget.

TikTok has the best reach per riyal but a small local audience, so it hits its cap at SAR 1,749. YouTube takes the rest; the other three stay at the SAR 300 minimum.

Extra money pays off only up to a point.

Each extra riyal reaches 0.23 more people until about SAR 6,134, when YouTube's cap binds too. Above SAR 7,823 there is no local audience left to buy.

The “be everywhere” rule costs reach.

Forcing SAR 300 onto every platform has a price: each riyal held on X or Instagram reaches about 0.1 fewer people than it would on YouTube.

Recommendation

  1. Run it for three monthsApply the split and track new customers per platform.
  2. Grow the budget moderatelyUp to about SAR 1,134 more keeps the full return per riyal.
  3. Re-optimize with real dataReplace estimated rates with the café's own campaign results.

Limits

Limits: Coefficients come from estimated local user counts and published ad rates, which change by season. The model maximizes reach, not profit.
