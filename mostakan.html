01 / The business problem

Not “what happened?” — what should the owner do next?

Mostakan Tea Room, an Al-Ahsa café established in May 2022, was experiencing declining orders and revenue across 2023–2025. The project focused on understanding the demand problem and turning a limited marketing budget into an allocation decision.

The analysis was grounded in the local Hofuf market and real operational constraints discussed with the café owner.

02 / Diagnose before optimizing

The decline came first.

Before allocating a single marketing riyal, the project examined sales and customer-volume patterns to establish the business problem. That matters: optimization only helps when the objective is tied to a real constraint and a real decision.

The resulting objective was clear—maximize projected customer acquisition while respecting the café's available marketing budget and platform limits.

04 / Optimization model

Make every riyal compete.

MAXIMIZE → projected customer acquisition
SUBJECT TO → budget + platform constraints

The linear-programming model translated the business owner's constraints into a structured allocation problem. Instead of spreading spend by intuition, each channel competed for budget based on its expected contribution while remaining inside the feasible limits.

06 / Why this case matters

No prediction for prediction’s sake.

The output is not a model score. It is a resource-allocation decision a business owner can actually use.
07 / Management takeaway

Use optimization to reduce guesswork—not to replace judgment.

The study used sensitivity analysis to explore how the solution changes as budget conditions move. Its recommendation favored a moderate budget increase within the tested range while maintaining diversification across channels, rather than blindly concentrating all spending in one place.

08 / Portfolio signal

Analytics can be small-business strategy.

This project demonstrates a different side of analytics from the healthcare cases: working directly from a business problem, combining operational evidence with constraints, and using optimization to support a managerial choice.

That makes the technical method secondary to the decision it enables—which is exactly the point.
