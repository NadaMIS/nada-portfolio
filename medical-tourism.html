Saudi Arabia Medical Tourism

Do international patients get the same care, at the same cost, as Saudi patients? I tested it on 79,000 hospital records before any recommendation on medical tourism.

  • Hypothesis testing
  • Classification
  • Strategy
79,000patient records compared
~1%difference in average cost
25%cost-band accuracy, the same as chance
59.7%best accuracy telling the groups apart (chance: 50%)

The problem

Vision 2030 names healthcare and tourism as growth sectors. If international patients were treated differently, with more tests, higher bills or worse outcomes, that would need fixing before any marketing. If they weren't, the barrier is access, not quality.

My role

Solo capstone for the M.S. in Business Analytics, supervised by Dr. Rasha Alahmad.

  • Python
  • SciPy
  • scikit-learn
  • imbalanced-learn
  • seaborn

How I approached it

  1. Comparet-tests on 8 clinical and cost variables
  2. Correlatewhat moves with total cost
  3. Predict cost3 classifiers on 4 equal cost bands
  4. Tell apartnationality classifier, undersampling vs SMOTE

What I found

The two groups are almost identical.

Lab tests and total cost differed statistically (p = 0.03) but by less than 1%. Age, stay and severity showed no difference, and about 70% recovered in both groups.

A model couldn't tell them apart.

The first model reached 79.8% by calling everyone Saudi. Once the classes were balanced, accuracy fell close to chance: clinical records don't distinguish international patients.

Individual cost can't be predicted from these records.

All three models scored about 25% on four equal cost bands, the same as guessing. The data supports comparing groups, not pricing single cases.

Recommendation

  1. Market it abroadPromote Saudi healthcare together with tourism boards.
  2. Remove the frictionA dedicated medical-visa pathway and integrated international insurance.
  3. Invest in specialist careTertiary centres for the complex cases patients travel for.

Limits

Limits: Several variables are almost perfectly uniform, which suggests simulated data, so the findings should be re-tested on verified records. Forecasting demand would need real monthly admissions by nationality.
