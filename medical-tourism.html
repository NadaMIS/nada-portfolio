Same care, same cost?

Before Saudi Arabia markets its hospitals to international patients, it needs evidence that those patients already get the same standard of care. I compared 79,000 hospital records for Saudi and non-Saudi patients to find out.

79,000inpatient records (63,046 Saudi, 15,954 non-Saudi)
~1%average cost difference (SAR 49,798 vs 50,174)
25%cost-class accuracy: the same as guessing
59.7%best accuracy telling the groups apart (chance: 50%)
Question

Vision 2030 names healthcare and tourism as growth sectors. If international patients were treated differently (more tests, higher bills, worse outcomes), that would have to be fixed before any marketing. If they weren't, the barrier is access, not quality.

Findings

The two groups are almost identical.

Age, stay, severity and comorbidities showed no significant difference. Lab tests and total cost did differ statistically (p = 0.03), but by less than 1%. Discharge outcomes matched too: about 70% recovered in both groups.

Non-Saudi vs Saudi patients · % difference in average

A model couldn't tell them apart.

I trained classifiers to predict nationality from 11 clinical features. The first reached 79.8%, but only by calling everyone Saudi. After balancing the classes, accuracy fell to 50.7% (undersampling) and 59.7% (SMOTE), close to chance.

Saudi vs non-Saudi classifier · test accuracy

Individual cost can't be predicted from these records.

Splitting cost into four equal bands, three models all scored about 25%, which is what guessing gives. Every clinical variable correlated with cost below 0.02. The data supports comparing groups, not pricing single cases.

Cost-band classifiers · accuracy (4 classes)
Recommendation
  1. Market it abroadPromote Saudi healthcare together with tourism boards and at international health-tourism events.
  2. Remove the frictionA dedicated medical-visa pathway and integrated international insurance.
  3. Invest in specialist careTertiary centres in Riyadh, Jeddah and Madinah for complex cases patients travel for.
My role

Solo capstone for the M.S. in Business Analytics, supervised by Dr. Rasha Alahmad: analysis design, statistics, modelling and recommendations.

Limits

Several variables are almost perfectly uniform, which suggests simulated data, so the findings should be re-tested on verified records. Forecasting demand would need real monthly admissions by nationality.
